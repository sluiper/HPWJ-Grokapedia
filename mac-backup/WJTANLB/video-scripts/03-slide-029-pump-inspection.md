## Spoken script (cleaned)

Welcome to our preoperation pump inspection training. In this comprehensive guide, we'll cover the steps necessary to ensure your high-pressure pump is prepared for safe and efficient operation. Remember, conducting these inspections prior to each use is essential for equipment integrity and operator safety. To start, always utilize a spotter to assist in moving the pump to the designated location for your task.

Once positioned, ensure the wheels are securely chocked to prevent any accidental movement during operation. If applicable, disconnect the pump trailer from the vehicle. Adjust the jack until the pump is level front to back. Also check that the pump is level side to side. If necessary, reposition the pump. Electrical safety is important. Utilize a grounding cable to ensure the pump is effectively grounded to the earth.

Once the ground cable is connected, use a multimeter to verify that the resistance is at an acceptable level. Moving on to fluid checks, inspect the diesel exhaust fluid or DEF tank to ensure there's an adequate supply for the task at hand. Check the fuel level and also the coolant level to prevent any interruptions during operation. Inspect the batteries to confirm they're fully charged and the battery boxes are shielding the batteries as designed.

Check the condition of belts, driveline, and other mechanically moving parts, ensuring all protective guards are securely in place. Check the engine oil level and condition, as well as the pump oil level. Check the grease fittings and add grease if needed. Safety features must be carefully inspected. Confirm that shrouds are correctly installed to cover any connections or hoses near the operator.

Make sure the fire extinguisher is accessible, has the proper rating, and is not expired. Verify the functionality of the pressure gauge and examine the rupture disk. Ensure it's appropriately tagged and rated for the pump's operating pressure and or the intended working pressure for the job. If not, promptly replace it with a suitable disk. Remember, the rupture disk burst rating is intended to be based on the system's desired operating pressure.

Open the water filter housing to check the status of the filter bag. Make sure the filter bag is clean and the micron rating is correct for the job. Get a new bag if necessary. Inspect the filter strainer to make sure it is clean and not obstructed. Replace the strainer and insert the clean filter bag. Then close the filter housing. Open the water tank and verify that all components are in working order, including the float, tank strainer, low water level sensor, and bypass water return.

Flush the water supply line to remove debris from the hose. Connect the supply hose and secure the connections as appropriate. Close the bottom valve of the filter assembly and open the top valve. Turn on the water supply to remove air from the filter assembly. Start the engine and check engine control readings, including the fuel level, diesel exhaust fluid level, engine hours, and battery level.

Open the bypass valve until it spins freely or check any other OEM indicator that marks fully open. With these comprehensive pre-operation checks completed, your high pressure pump is now fully prepared for the task at hand. Remember, attention to detail during inspection leads to safety and efficiency. Thank you for watching.

## Review points

- Checklist order: spotter/move → chock/level → ground/multimeter → DEF/fuel/coolant/batteries → belts/guards → engine oil + pump oil → grease → shrouds → fire extinguisher → pressure gauge + rupture disk (tagged to **job** working pressure, not just nameplate) → filter bag + strainer → water tank (float, strainer, low-level sensor, bypass return) → flush supply → fill/bleed filter → start engine → open bypass until it spins freely.
- Rupture-disk rating = **desired system operating pressure**, not “whatever is on the pump.”
