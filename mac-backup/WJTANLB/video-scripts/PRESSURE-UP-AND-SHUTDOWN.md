# Pressure-up and shutdown — step by step

From **engine start** to **working pressure**, then from **dump** to **pump off**.

This is a two-person sequence. The **nozzle / gun operator owns pressure**. The **pump operator never increases pressure without a clear signal** from the person on the tool.

| Role | Job during this sequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Gun / lance operator** | Dump, stance, signals, leak check. Person closest to the nozzle has the dump / dead-man / foot valve. |
| **Pump operator** | Engine, bypass, regulator. Watches the gun operator. Never walks pressure up on their own. |
| **Whole crew** | Same three hand signals, line of sight, stop-work on comms loss. |

**Sources:** WJTA 2024 generic videos (pump inspection, shotgun, automated tube); WJTA FV checklists (Man Shotgun / Tube / Linemole / Auto Tube, Pre-Job Pump walk); Anabeeb OPS-P-019 §9; Grokapedia Ch8 / Ch11 (MCR-011, MCR-025, MCR-037).

## The three WJTA hand signals

Every person on the job must demonstrate these **before** the engine starts:

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Turn on water** | Low-pressure water / flush / start of the ladder |
| **Pressure up / OK to begin blasting** | Increase (to half, then to full). OK to work once full is confirmed |
| **Cut pressure** | Dump and shut the pump down |

Anabeeb also requires agreed signals for **start / increase / decrease / shutdown**. Brief the three WJTA signals plus: dump/kill (hand across throat), hold/do not increase (fist up), emergency stop (arms crossed overhead).

**Line of sight is mandatory.** If you lose sight or radio, dump immediately. Do not guess.

---

## Gates before anyone starts the engine

If any of these fail, **do not start**.

1. Pump walk complete (level, chocked, grounded, guards on, rupture disc tagged to **this job’s** working pressure, filter clean, gauges live, extinguisher tagged).
2. Hoses, fittings, shrouds, whip-checks, nozzles rated and inspected. Colour / MAWP match the system.
3. Barricade up (WJTA shotgun: ≥15 ft / 4.5 m; Anabeeb: unauthorised persons out to **10 m**).
4. PPE on the nozzle operator (hard hat, glasses, face shield, hearing, slicker, PVC gloves, metatarsal; double hearing within ~35 ft of the pump).
5. Dump / dead-man / foot pedal in the **hands of the person at the nozzle**. Trigger never locked, wedged, or blocked ON.
6. Method safety devices tested **before pressure**: shotgun shroud and cartridge; tube AWD collet (pull-test — crimp cannot pass); line-mole stinger / back-out (rigid length ≥ 1.5× ID); auto hose-stop and collet pull-test.
7. Thrust known for this orifice / P / Q. Handheld: ≤ 1/3 body weight **and** ≤ 60 lbf / 250 N.
8. Hand signals practiced. Pump operator knows they wait.

## Part A — Start the pump until you are at working pressure

Do these in order. Do not skip a rung. **Increase slowly.**

### A0. Water in, air out (pump still not loaded)

**Pump operator**

1. Suction / supply hose flushed of debris, then connected.
2. Filter: bottom valve closed, top valve open; water on; bleed air from the filter housing until water is solid (no bubbles).
3. Water tank: float, tank strainer, low-level sensor, bypass return all working.
4. **Bypass / unloader fully open** (spins freely, or OEM “fully open” mark). The pump must start **unloaded**.

Do not start a dry or air-locked fluid end.

### A1. Start the engine — still unloaded

**Pump operator**

5. Start engine.
6. Read the panel: fuel, DEF, hours, battery, oil, temperature. Anything wrong → shut down, do not continue.
7. Confirm bypass still fully open. Engine is running; **system is not at pressure**.

The gun is still cold. Nozzle is **not** on yet.

### A2. Flush the high-pressure side — nozzle OFF

Debris in a fitted nozzle becomes a projectile and a plugged jet (wrong thrust, wrong pattern).

**Gun / lance operator**

8. Nozzle **removed**.
9. Shotgun: rest the barrel on the workpiece, a beam, or structure so you are not holding the weight.
10. Tube / auto: lance in the AWD / tractor; pull-test the collet **before** asking for water.
11. Signal **Turn on water**.

**Pump operator**

12. Bring **low-pressure** water only. Do not rack the regulator.

**Gun / lance operator**

13. Open the dump (trigger / foot pedal / dump button) so water runs through the hose and out the open lance.
14. Confirm a solid stream, no grit burst, no milky air.
15. Signal **Cut pressure**.

**Pump operator**

16. Shut the pump / dump to zero. Wait.

**Gun / lance operator**

17. Fit the nozzle. Anti-seize on threads/cone. Open-end wrench only (no pipe wrench, no pliers).
18. Shotgun: re-secure the burst shroud over the whip connections.
19. Tube: insert nozzle **at least 6 in** into the tube **before** the dump is used to pressurise.
20. Line mole: insert tool **at least 24 in** into the pipe **before** the dump is used to pressurise. Hose marked at 2 ft / 4 ft / 6 ft from the tool.
21. Auto: hose stop on; Banshee past the rear sheet ½ in / 13 mm; collet pull-test again.

### A3. Low pressure — prove the dump

This is the first pressurised step. Jet is live. Stance on.

**Gun / lance operator**

22. PPE confirmed. Stance: feet shoulder-width, staggered; stock in the shoulder (shotgun).
23. **Lock the barrel** under a pipe or structure if you can, so the gun is not free in your hands during pressure-up.
24. Nozzle **pointed away from the work and away from people**.
25. Signal **Turn on water**.
26. Engage dump (trigger in / pedal down / dump closed so water goes to the nozzle).
27. Watch: dump **closes**, water **reroutes** to the lance, no spray at the dump body.
28. If that fails → dump immediately, **Cut pressure**, do not continue.

**Pump operator**

29. Low pressure only. Stay on the gun operator. Do not look at your phone.

### A4. Half pressure — leaks and dump again

**Gun / lance operator**

30. Disengage dump (trigger out / pedal up) so the system dumps.
31. Signal **Pressure up** — meaning **half** the intended working pressure.
32. Re-engage dump. Wait for the pump operator’s signal that half pressure is on the gauge.
33. Walk the hose with your eyes: connections, shroud, whip-checks, gun body. **Any leak → dump, Cut pressure, repair only at zero.** Never tighten a joint under pressure.
34. Test dump at half: release trigger/pedal, confirm pressure falls and the jet dies. Re-engage. Dump must work **before** you go to full.

### A5. Full working pressure — feel it, then work

**Gun / lance operator**

35. Disengage dump.
36. Signal **Pressure up** — **full** intended working pressure.
37. Re-engage dump. **Hold the trigger / pedal until the pump operator has set the regulator.** Do not let go mid-set (the checklist item: *maintain pressure on the trigger until pump pressure has been set*).
38. Shotgun: still pointed **off the work**. Take the proper stance. Feel the thrust. If it is more than you briefed (1/3 body weight / 60 lbf / 250 N) → dump, Cut pressure, change orifice or P/Q. Do not “see how it goes.”
39. Auto / tube: dump stays activated until the gauge is at the set point, then **test dump once** before the first cleaning pass.
40. Only now: reposition onto the work. Signal / thumbs-up that you are at pressure and starting.

**Pump operator**

41. Set the regulator to the **agreed** working pressure — not “what the pump can do.” Rupture disc is already tagged to this number.
42. Hands off the up-button unless the gun operator signals again.
43. Watch the operator, the zone, and the gauge. Intruder in the 10 m / barricade → you dump / E-stop even if they have not signalled.

You are now at pressure. Work in 12–18 in squares (shotgun). Release trigger to dump whenever you need to move, talk, or rest.

## Part B — Lower pressure until the pump is off

Never reverse this order. Never slam a valve (water-hammer). Never walk away from a live gun.

### B1. Dump first — you, on the tool

**Gun / lance operator**

44. Release trigger / lift foot pedal / open dump. Jet dies. Hose goes slack.
45. Confirm dump is **open** and staying open. Do not rest a finger on a live trigger “just in case.”

### B2. Tell the pump to come down

**Gun / lance operator**

46. Signal **Cut pressure**. Keep **line of sight** with the pump operator until they acknowledge.
47. **Do not leave the gun / lance.** Hands on it.

**Pump operator**

48. Bring the regulator down in a controlled way (decrease signal if you are ramping, not an emergency).
49. Open bypass / unloader fully.
50. Gauge to **zero**. If the needle hangs, the dump is not open or there is a trapped volume — do not assume it is dead.

### B3. Engine off

**Pump operator**

51. Only after zero: shut the engine down (diesel cool-down if the OEM requires it; isolate electric if electric).
52. E-stop the auto panel if one is in use.

**Gun / lance operator**

53. Stay on the tool until the engine is **actually stopped**, not just “he said he would.” WJTA: *do not leave the gun unattended until the pump is completely shut down.*

### B4. Prove it is dead, then you may let go

Both

54. Gauge reads zero.
55. Dump / dead-man is open and verified.
56. Hoses have no residual kick. Feel them.
57. **Then** you may unhand the gun, pull the nozzle (tube: nozzle out **after** depressurising, never before), and only then change tips, crack fittings, or walk off.

### B5. If you are leaving the unit or doing any work on it

58. LOTO / isolate (MCR-026). Dump valve is **not** isolation for maintenance.
59. Caps on open ends. Hoses secured.
60. Barricades stay up until the system is dead and the client agrees they can come down.

## If it goes wrong (cut in at any step)

| Event | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Leak | Dump → Cut pressure → zero → then repair. Never tighten live. |
| Lost line of sight / radio | Dump immediately. Do not wait for a better signal. |
| Unauthorised person inside 10 m / barricade | Dump and depressurise. Do not keep working “they’re just walking through.” |
| Dump does not kill the jet | Pump operator E-stop / kill engine. Treat as emergency. Do not keep triggering to “see.” |
| Injection injury / hit | Dump + engine kill first. Then first aid. Time is tissue. |
| Fatigue / lost stance | Dump, Cut pressure, rotate. Do not finish the square. |

## Method differences (same ladder, different lock-down)

| Method | Before first dump-to-pressure | During pressure-up | Pressure down |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Manual shotgun** | Barrel locked under structure; shroud on; nozzle off-work | Hold trigger until pump is **set**; dump-test at low and half; feel thrust at full **off the work** | Release trigger → Cut pressure → hold the gun until engine is dead |
| **Manual tube** | AWD collet pull-test; nozzle **≥6 in** into the tube | Foot pedal dump; LOS; dump-test before first tube | Release pedal → Cut pressure → hold the lance until engine is dead |
| **Line mole** | Back-out / stinger pull-test; tool **≥24 in** in; marks at 2/4/6 ft | Same as tube | Same as tube |
| **Automated tube** | Collet + hose stop; controls out of the jet; dump on the panel | Dump **held on** until set pressure; dump-test; then index | Dump + Cut pressure; stay on the panel until pump is dead |

## One-page card (print this)

**UP**

1. Bypass open → start engine → gauges good.  
2. Flush, **nozzle off** → Cut pressure → fit nozzle.  
3. Turn on water (low) → dump works, water reroutes.  
4. Pressure up to **half** → leaks? dump-test.  
5. Pressure up to **full** → hold dump until pump is set → feel thrust off the work.  
6. Then clean.

**DOWN**

1. Dump (trigger / pedal / button).  
2. Signal **Cut pressure** (LOS).  
3. Regulator down, bypass open, **gauge zero**.  
4. Engine off.  
5. Hands stay on the gun until the engine is stopped.  
6. Only then change nozzles or walk away.

**Never:** increase without a signal · slam valves · lock the trigger ON · tighten a live joint · leave a live gun · start the engine with bypass shut · pressurise with the nozzle in your hand in open air.
